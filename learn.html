Stage 5 · Learn

Every run is evidence. Trellis turns it into changes a person approves.

Learning here means the system improves its own graph, prompts, model choices, skills and even its policy, based on what its runs show. It does not mean model weights changing in production. Every proposal arrives with before-and-after eval results, and nothing reaches production without a person and a canary.

Signals

Not all feedback is equal

A thumbs-down is cheap to collect and easy to misread. An approver who edits the refund amount before approving has just written a labelled example. Trellis weighs signals by how much they can be trusted.

SignalHow it is collectedStrengthWatch out for
Approver edits and rejectionsEvery approval card records approve, edit or reject, with the diffstrongApprovers who rubber-stamp. Watch approval time and rate per approver.
Downstream outcomesTicket reopened within 7 days, PR comment resolved or dismissed, incident recurredstrongDelay: outcomes arrive days later and need joining back to the run.
Eval scoresDeterministic checks plus model-graded rubrics on sampled runsmediumJudge bias. Calibrate graders against human labels every month.
TracesCost, latency, retries, failures, guardrail trips per nodeobjectiveSays what was slow or expensive, not whether the answer was right.
Thumbs and commentsEnd users rate a reply or a reportweakSparse and skewed toward unhappy users. Never used alone.

Keeping learning safe

          
Rules the learning loop itself must follow

        

        

          
Never straight to production
Every change passes the eval gate, then a canary on a share of traffic, then a person promotes it. Rollback is one click and keeps the old version warm.

          
Held-out sets stay held out
Cases used to mine patterns or tune prompts are never used to judge them. Each system keeps a frozen test set that only grows.

          
Resist poisoned feedback
Signals are weighted by source. One user's 200 thumbs-downs count once. Proposals need agreement across signal types.

          
Policy changes go to policy owners
Trellis can say a rule looks too strict. Only the rule's owner can change it, and the change is audited like any other.

        

        
Where fine-tuning fits
The same datasets can later train a small model for a high-volume node, such as intent classification once there are 20,000 labelled tickets. That is a separate, later track. The eval harness built here is what makes it safe to try.
